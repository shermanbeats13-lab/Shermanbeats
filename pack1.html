How to Run Your Mystery Evening

🕯️ Setting the Mood — before guests arrive

  • •Dim the lights and light candles (or use warm fairy lights). No overhead lighting during the mystery.
  • •Play an ambient playlist: "Rainy evening jazz" or "Dark academia study music" on Spotify.
  • •Set out wine, charcuterie, hot cocoa — whatever feels cozy and indulgent.
  • •Print the Evidence Pack and place items in a manila envelope labeled "CEDAR RIDGE — RESTRICTED FILES"
  • •Print the Investigation Worksheet for both players.
  • •Optional: Write each character name on a card and place face-down — reveal when the game begins.

🌙 Flow of the Evening

ESTIMATED: 2–2.5 HOURS

Read "The Setup" together aloud from the Story & Characters tab.

💡 Host Tips

  • Stay in the mood — speak in hushed, conspiratorial tones throughout.
  • Award a "Detective of the Evening" title to whoever got closest to the truth.
  • The mystery is designed so both players can reach different conclusions — that's intentional and makes the reveal more fun!

Tab 2

The Silent Hour at Cedar Ridge

The Premise

On the stormy night of October 15th, renowned landscape painter Dominic Frey was found dead in the locked study of Cedar Ridge Lodge — a remote lakeside retreat nestled in the forests above Shawnigan Lake, BC.

The cause: a single blow to the back of the head. The study was locked from the inside. The phone lines were cut. And the four guests staying at the lodge that weekend each had something to hide.

It's been 48 hours since the body was found. Local detective work has stalled. YOU have been called in to review the case files and interview the witnesses.

One of the four people at Cedar Ridge that night is a killer.

DECEASED

The Victim

Dominic Frey, 52

Celebrated but controversial landscape painter; recently turned to writing a tell-all memoir

Known for: Charisma, manipulation, and knowing everyone's secrets

Found: Locked study, 11:47 PM — blunt force trauma

Discovery: The locked door was broken down the following morning by lodge owner Talia Rowan

The Suspects

Four guests. Four secrets. One killer.

🕵️

Elena Marrow, 38

THE ARCHIVIST

Background: Dominic's former research assistant and rumored long-term affair partner. Arrived under the pretense of a "professional reconciliation" — she claimed Dominic had stolen research from her that formed the backbone of his most famous work, "The Crow Shore Series."

Secret: Elena had secretly drafted a legal cease-and-desist letter she planned to serve Dominic that weekend. She also found a letter in her room, apparently from Dominic, that read: "It ends this weekend. All of it."

Alibi: Claims she was in her room reading from 10 PM onward. No witnesses.

Demeanor: Precise, guarded, hyper-observant. Rarely shows emotion but watches everyone.

🎻

Jonas Hale, 45

THE COMPOSER

Background: Dominic's oldest friend — known each other since art school. Jonas composed scores for several documentary films Dominic directed. Recently discovered Dominic planned to include damaging personal revelations about Jonas's mental health history and a long-hidden affair in his memoir.

Secret: Overheard on the phone the evening of the murder saying, "If he publishes it, I'll lose everything." Refuses to say who he was speaking with.

Alibi: Claims he was in the music room playing piano until midnight. Piano was heard until approximately 11:15 PM.

Demeanor: Warm on the surface, but increasingly erratic. Prone to dramatic silences and deflection.

🏡

Marin Kessler, 61

THE LODGE HISTORIAN

Background: Retired academic and local historian documenting the history of Cedar Ridge Lodge for a book project. Known to Dominic through mutual literary circles. Dominic's memoir contains a chapter implicating Marin's late husband in a decades-old financial scandal.

Secret: Seen entering the east corridor — which leads to the study — at approximately 11:30 PM. Claims she was looking for the kitchen. The east corridor does not lead to the kitchen.

Alibi: Claims she returned to her room after "a brief walk." Cannot account for 40 minutes.

Demeanor: Calm, grandmotherly, disarmingly pleasant. Possibly the most dangerous kind of deceiver.

🔑

Talia Rowan, 34

THE LODGE OWNER

Background: Inherited Cedar Ridge from her mother and has been trying to keep it solvent for years. Dominic promised to feature the lodge in his memoir as a "jewel of BC's artistic heritage" — but recently threatened to write a scathing exposé instead, citing mold, structural issues, and alleged negligence.

Secret: Talia broke open the study door the morning the body was found — but a smudged partial handprint suggests the door may have been opened and re-locked at least once before.

Alibi: Claims she was doing late-night maintenance checks until 11:30 PM, then went to bed. The maintenance log is suspiciously neat.

Demeanor: Anxious, over-helpful, slightly too eager to assist the investigation.

Tab 3

Cedar Ridge — Restricted Case Files

Review each piece of evidence carefully. Discuss your theories.

File #CR-001 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 1 — The Locked Room

The study door was locked from the inside using a sliding bolt. However, the lodge's original blueprints (found in Marin's research files) reveal a narrow ventilation panel above the study's east wall — approximately 14 inches wide. An adult could not pass through it, but a long tool or arm could potentially reach the bolt. The panel cover was found slightly askew the morning of the discovery.

File #CR-002 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 2 — The Shattered Whiskey Glass

A crystal whiskey glass was found shattered on the study's hardwood floor, roughly 3 feet from the body. Analysis shows two sets of fingerprints: Dominic's (expected) and one partial print, smudged beyond identification. The whiskey inside was laced with a mild sedative — not enough to kill, but enough to impair. The lodge's sedative medications (prescribed to Jonas Hale for sleep) were found short by two doses.

File #CR-003 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 3 — The Torn Letter

Found in the fireplace grate, partially burned. Reconstructed text reads:

"...you cannot publish what you do not understand. I have the originals. If you proceed, I will have no choice but to go to the police about [burned section] — and this time I will not stay silent. Meet me at [burned section] at eleven. Come alone."

The handwriting analysis is inconclusive — consistent with Elena Marrow's known samples, but also consistent with Marin Kessler's.

File #CR-004 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 4 — The Piano Recording

Lodge security camera (exterior only) captured audio bleed from the music room window. Timestamp analysis shows piano music from 10:08 PM to 11:14 PM. At 11:14 PM, the music stops abruptly mid-phrase. Jonas claims he played until midnight. A 46-minute gap is unaccounted for. The camera shows the music room light turned back on at 11:58 PM.

File #CR-005 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 5 — The Maintenance Log

Talia's handwritten maintenance log records checks at 9:00 PM, 10:00 PM, and 11:00 PM. However, forensic ink analysis suggests all three entries were written at the same time — the ink bleed and pen pressure are identical across all three timestamps. The 11:00 PM entry notes "east corridor lighting — all clear." But the east corridor motion sensor logged activity at 11:32 PM — after Talia claims she was in bed.

File #CR-006 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 6 — Dominic's Open Laptop

The laptop was open on Dominic's desk, screen still lit. The final document open was Chapter 22 of his memoir, titled "The Sins of Cedar Ridge." Last edit timestamp: 11:03 PM. The final paragraph:

"She thought I'd forgotten. But I never forget. The photograph proves everything, and I intend to present it in full as Appendix C of this work. Let the reader decide who the real criminal is."

No photograph was found anywhere in the study.

File #CR-007 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 7 — The Missing Photograph

Lodge guests' rooms were searched (with consent). No photograph matching the memoir's description was found. However, Elena Marrow's luggage contained a torn photo frame — empty — with cedar sap residue on the back, suggesting it had been removed from a tree or outdoor surface. Talia reported that a framed photograph had been displayed on a cedar post near the lodge's east trail — it had been removed sometime in the past week.

File #CR-008 EVIDENCE

Evidence Item 8 — The Ventilation Panel Tool

A 36-inch fireplace poker from the lodge's east sitting room was found to have a small bent end consistent with having been used to push or pull a sliding bolt. No fingerprints — wiped clean. Marin Kessler's room is the closest room to the east sitting room.

Tab 4

Witness Interviews

One of you plays Detective. The other reads the witness role. Switch for each interview.

Read these aloud, in character. The witness can only answer what's written — but can add nervous hesitation, sighs, or dramatic pauses for atmosphere!

🕵️

Interview 1: Elena Marrow

CEDAR RIDGE INVESTIGATION — WITNESS TRANSCRIPT

DETECTIVE:

Ms. Marrow, how would you describe your relationship with Dominic Frey?

ELENA:

[long pause] Professional. Once. Then complicated. Then over. I came here to end things cleanly — legally. I did not come here to end him.

DETECTIVE:

We found a letter in your luggage mentioning "the originals." What originals?

ELENA:

[stiffens] Research. Field notes. Dominic built his reputation on work that was half mine. I wanted them back, or at least acknowledged. That's not a motive. That's justice.

DETECTIVE:

Where were you between 11 PM and midnight?

ELENA:

In my room. Alone. Reading. I know how that sounds.

DETECTIVE:

Did you write a letter to Dominic asking him to meet you that night?

ELENA:

[very still] No. But I think someone wanted it to look like I did.

🎻

Interview 2: Jonas Hale

CEDAR RIDGE INVESTIGATION — WITNESS TRANSCRIPT

DETECTIVE:

Mr. Hale, you were heard saying "if he publishes it, I'll lose everything." What did you mean?

JONAS:

[laughs softly, then stops] My wife doesn't know about a period in my life I'm not proud of. Dominic was going to put it in print. In detail. With names.

DETECTIVE:

The piano music in your alibi stops at 11:14 PM — nearly an hour before you claim to have stopped playing. Can you explain that?

JONAS:

[long silence] I... stepped out. For air. I was emotional. Being here, knowing what Dominic planned to publish... I needed a moment.

DETECTIVE:

Where did you go when you "stepped out"?

JONAS:

The back garden. The lake path. I didn't go near the study. I swear to you.

DETECTIVE:

Did you take any of your sleep medication that night?

JONAS:

[quietly] One. I took one. I only have one left, not two missing. Someone must have taken the other.

🏡

Interview 3: Marin Kessler

CEDAR RIDGE INVESTIGATION — WITNESS TRANSCRIPT

DETECTIVE:

Mrs. Kessler, a motion sensor places you in the east corridor at 11:32 PM. You said you were looking for the kitchen. The kitchen is in the west wing.

MARIN:

[warm smile] I'm 61 and I was in an unfamiliar lodge at night without my reading glasses. I got turned around. Surely that's not criminal.

DETECTIVE:

Dominic's memoir apparently implicated your late husband in a financial scandal. How did you feel about that?

MARIN:

[pause] My husband was a good man who made one mistake and spent the rest of his life making it right. Dominic had no right — no right — to drag that into public discourse after Harold is gone and cannot defend himself.

DETECTIVE:

Did you write a letter to Dominic that night?

MARIN:

I did not. But I know who might have wanted someone to think I did.

DETECTIVE:

Who?

MARIN:

[smiles gently] That's what you're here to find out, isn't it?

🔑

Interview 4: Talia Rowan

CEDAR RIDGE INVESTIGATION — WITNESS TRANSCRIPT

DETECTIVE:

Ms. Rowan, your maintenance log entries appear to have been written all at once, not in real time. Can you explain?

TALIA:

[nervous laugh] I... I write them up at the end of the night sometimes. From memory. I know I should be more careful but the lodge is just me, there's no staff, I —

DETECTIVE:

The east corridor motion sensor logged activity at 11:32 PM. Your log says you were in bed by 11:30. Which is true?

TALIA:

[long pause] I... may have done one more check. I forgot to include it.

DETECTIVE:

A partial handprint near the study door suggests it may have been opened and re-locked before you broke it down. Is that possible?

TALIA:

[very quietly] I don't know anything about that.

DETECTIVE:

Ms. Rowan — did Dominic actually have damaging information about the lodge's structural issues? Enough to shut you down?

TALIA:

[eyes fill] He had photographs. He had reports. He would have destroyed everything my mother built. Everything I've spent ten years trying to save.

Tab 7

The Truth Behind The Silent Hour

CEDAR RIDGE — CLASSIFIED

THE KILLER IS

Talia Rowan

THE LODGE OWNER

The Full Truth

Dominic Frey did not plan to feature Cedar Ridge Lodge in his memoir — he planned to destroy it. He had obtained a damning structural engineer's report showing the lodge had serious safety violations that Talia had concealed from guests for years. Combined with photographs of visible mold and decay, his chapter "The Sins of Cedar Ridge" would have triggered regulatory investigation, potential lawsuits, and public outrage that would have ended the lodge forever.

But the final straw was personal: the photograph displayed on the cedar post on the east trail was a photo of Talia's mother — a photo Dominic had stolen and intended to use as the cover of the lodge exposé chapter, captioned with a mocking reference to a legacy "built on negligence." For Talia, this wasn't about money. It was about her mother's memory.

How She Did It

Talia had a copy of every key at Cedar Ridge — including a master key that could operate the study's interior bolt via a thin access tool through the ventilation panel, a feature she'd always known about as the lodge owner. She forged a letter to appear as though it came from Elena — counting on the existing animosity between them to make Elena a plausible suspect.

She retrieved Jonas's sleep medication from his unlocked room earlier that day, slipped a dose into Dominic's whiskey (which she served him as the lodge host), waited for it to take partial effect, then entered the study while he was groggy. The confrontation escalated. She struck him with a bronze bookend — not found because she dropped it in the lodge's old well, accessed from the east trail.

She locked the study bolt from the outside using the fireplace poker through the ventilation panel — leaving it in the east sitting room (a mistake: she wiped it but forgot it entirely). She then created the false maintenance log entries to account for her movements. In the morning, she was the one who "found" the locked door and broke it down — destroying any subtle evidence of the bolt mechanism in the process.

The Red Herrings

•

Elena's letter was a forgery — Talia copied her handwriting from lodge guest registration cards

•

Jonas's missing piano hour was real but innocent — he walked to the lake, grief-stricken, and didn't see anything

•

Marin's east corridor walk was genuine disorientation — but her calm demeanor made her suspicious

📸 The Photograph

Talia removed it from the cedar post herself, three days before the murder weekend — she couldn't bear the thought of it appearing in his book. It was found, later, tucked beneath the mattress in her room.
